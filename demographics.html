How it spread

  • 1950s: Dianetics becomes a bestseller, and groups form to practice Hubbard's techniques. A Dianetics "fad" lasts several years and attracts intellectuals such as Aldous Huxley. (Lewis)
  • 1970s: The Church spreads across Europe and translates Hubbard's writings into many languages. (Melton)
  • 1980s–90s: Growth continues, and after the fall of the Berlin Wall the Church spreads into eastern Europe. (Melton)
  • Today: The Church operates in more than 150 countries. (Melton)

Who counts as a Scientologist?

Counting members depends first on how a "Scientologist" is defined. Introvigne describes the movement as a set of circles: a core of members for whom Scientology is their main religious identity, a wider circle who visit churches regularly, and a larger circle who visit only occasionally. (Introvigne)

This problem is not unique to Scientology. In Italy, for example, almost 98% of the population has been baptized Catholic, but only about 18% regularly attend Mass. (Introvigne)

What the surveys found

The American Religious Identification Survey (ARIS) estimated the number of U.S. Scientologists in 1990 and 2001. Its 2008 survey did not publish a figure at all, because the numbers for small religions were too unreliable. Asked informally, the survey's lead author gave only a very wide range. (Introvigne)

The "25,000" figure

A widely repeated claim says ARIS found only 25,000 Scientologists in 2008. According to Introvigne, this number traces back to a 2009 post on an anti-Scientology message board, which reported an informal answer from the ARIS team while warning that the data were unreliable. It then appeared in a 2010 New York Times article, spread to Wikipedia, and was cited by scholars as fact. (Introvigne) ARIS's lead author, Barry Kosmin, later explained that the informal estimate was "25,000 +/- 300,000." (Introvigne)

The Church's side and outside counts

  • The Italian Church of Scientology ran its own census in 2006 and published the results in 2011. Introvigne finds its method persuasive, though the data are now old. (Introvigne)
  • A 2014 study by Florida State University scholars, sponsored by the Church, found that the number of Scientologists moving to or visiting the Clearwater, Florida, area was substantial and growing. Critics dismissed it because the Church funded it and supplied the figures. (Introvigne)
  • Critics and former members often argue that membership is shrinking, but Introvigne cautions that they frequently generalize from small amounts of data. (Introvigne)
  • General surveys like ARIS, which interviewed 54,461 people in 2008, are too small to measure religions with few members accurately. (Introvigne)

The true number remains unknown. Introvigne concludes that an accurate count would require the cooperation of the Church, independent auditors, and scholars. (Introvigne)

Well-known members

Celebrities who have embraced Scientology's teachings include Tom Cruise, John Travolta, and Kirstie Alley. (Melton)
