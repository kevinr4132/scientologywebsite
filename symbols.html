Symbols

Eight points for the eight dynamics of survival

Scientology's best-known symbol is the eight-pointed cross. Each point stands for one of the eight dynamics, the ever-larger parts of life a person learns to identify with. (Melton)

The eight dynamics

A Scientologist first feels the urge to survive as an individual, then learns to identify with larger and larger realities. (Melton)

  1. SelfThe urge to survive as an individual
  2. FamilySurvival through one's family
  3. GroupThe tribe or nation
  4. HumankindAll of humanity
  5. Life formsThe entire animal kingdom
  6. Physical universeMatter, energy, space, and time (MEST)
  7. SpiritsThe spiritual universe
  8. InfinityInfinity, or God

All eight dynamics as described in Melton.

The E-meter

Another recognizable image of Scientology is the E-meter, the instrument used in auditing. Hubbard presented it as a modern technology able to reveal a person's hidden emotions. (Urban)

A debated origin

Scholar Hugh Urban argues that early Scientology borrowed ideas from the occultist Aleister Crowley, a link the Church has firmly denied since it was first suggested in 1969. (Urban)

If one of your sources says the cross itself was modeled on an occult order's cross, add that here with the citation. It's not in your research document.

On the sign outside

The sign at the Atlanta church shows another Scientology symbol: a letter S woven through two triangles.
