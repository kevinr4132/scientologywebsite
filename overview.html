Overview

An international religious movement that emerged in the 1950s

Scientology is an international movement that emerged in the 1950s from the thought of L. Ron Hubbard, a writer who first shared his ideas with the public in Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health (1950). (Melton)

Hubbard's goal was to understand what troubles the human mind and to offer a way to overcome it. Over time he moved beyond Dianetics' focus on the mind toward a spiritual approach to the human condition, which he called Scientology. The Church of Scientology was founded in 1954. (Melton)

The big idea

Scientology teaches that a person's true self is the thetan, an immortal spiritual being that has lived before and can exist apart from the body. Through counseling called auditing, members clear away painful past experiences and climb toward becoming an Operating Thetan, a fully aware and capable spiritual being. (Melton)

Science or religion?

Hubbard described his work in the language of science rather than faith, presenting it as something that could be demonstrated and applied. (Lewis) Scholars still debate how to classify it: James R. Lewis argues that Hubbard turned his movement into a religion largely for practical reasons, yet concludes that it became a religion nevertheless. (Lewis)
