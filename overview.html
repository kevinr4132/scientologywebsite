Overview

A movement that began with one book and became a worldwide church

Scientology is an international movement that emerged in the 1950s from the thought of L. Ron Hubbard, a writer who first shared his ideas with the public in Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health (1950). (Melton)

Hubbard's goal was to understand what troubles the human mind and to offer a way to overcome it. Over time he moved beyond Dianetics' focus on the mind toward a spiritual approach to the human condition, which he called Scientology. The Church of Scientology was founded in 1954. (Melton)

The big idea

Scientology teaches that your true self is the thetan, an immortal spiritual being that has lived before and can exist apart from the body. Through counseling called auditing, members clear away painful past experiences and climb toward becoming an Operating Thetan, a fully aware and capable spiritual being. (Melton)

Science of the mind, religion of the spirit

Hubbard described his work in the language of science rather than faith, presenting it as something that could be tested and applied. (Lewis) Members see Scientology the same way today: not just something to believe, but a practical set of tools for improving their lives.

“We're not playing some minor game in Scientology. ... your own destiny for the next endless trillions of years depend on what you do here and now with and in Scientology.”

L. Ron Hubbard, 1965, quoted in Lewis
