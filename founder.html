Founder

L. Ron Hubbard: writer, explorer, naval officer, and founder

Before he founded a religion, L. Ron Hubbard lived the life of an adventurer, and it shaped everything he would later teach.

Writer and explorer

Hubbard attended George Washington University in Washington, D.C., from 1930 to 1932, then left to pursue other interests. He married in 1933 and became a popular writer for pulp magazines, publishing westerns, horror, and science fiction. He was also an explorer: in 1940 he was elected to the Explorer's Club, and he earned licenses as a Master of Steam and Motor Vessels and Master of Sail Vessels. Ships would later play a central role in the Church. (Melton)

War and a search for answers

During World War II, Hubbard served in naval intelligence in Australia and aboard ships off the U.S. coast. He ended the war as a patient at Oak Knoll Naval Hospital in Oakland, California, and it was there that he began thinking seriously about the problems of the human mind. After the war he set out on a personal quest for a "science of the mind." (Melton)

Life of the founder

  1. 1930
    Enters George Washington University, leaving in 1932 without a degree. (Melton)
  2. 1940
    Elected to the Explorer's Club. (Melton)
  3. 1941–45
    Serves in naval intelligence during World War II, ending the war at Oak Knoll Naval Hospital. (Melton)
  4. 1948
    Writes The Original Thesis, his first statement of his ideas. (Melton)
  5. 1950
    Publishes Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health, which becomes a bestseller. (Lewis)
  6. 1951
    Announces Scientology, which deals with the human spirit, not only the mind. (Lewis)
  7. 1954
    The Church of Scientology is founded. (Melton)
  8. 1966
    Resigns all offices in the Church to develop the advanced OT levels, much of it aboard the ship Apollo. (Melton)
  9. 1986
    Hubbard dies. (Lewis)
